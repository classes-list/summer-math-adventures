PARENT & ADMIN

Frequently Asked Questions

What grade levels is this for?

Rising 4th and 5th graders. Mixed-level crews work well because older students mentor younger.

How many students per session?

12–20 explorers, in crews of 3–4. One head teacher plus one assistant works best.

How much math vs. play?

Roughly 60% direct math practice, 40% story/game framing. The framing is the carrot — the math is the meal.

What if a student misses a day?

Each day's mission stands alone. They can complete the week's boss challenge with crew support.

Is there homework?

It's summer — minimal. One short adventure journal entry per night.

What does my child take home?

Journal, explorer pack, five map pieces, five mission badges, plus the Captain badge.

HOW IT WORKS

One Crew, Three Phases, Full Attention

With four explorers, you don't need rotating stations. Run the whole 35 minutes as one continuous Adventure Workshop with the same crew, transitioning through three different activity formats. You're present for all of it.

Phase 1 — Game: All four explorers play the day's game together at one table. The competition still works perfectly with 4 players.

Phase 2 — Hands-on Build: Each explorer works with their own materials. You float between them, asking questions.

Phase 3 — Coach & Challenge: You lead the whole crew through harder problems together — deep small-group coaching.

Counting the Shipwreck Supplies

Place value through hundred-thousands
🏝️
1
10 min Game

Place Value War 🃏

Materials

  • 1 deck of cards (Ace-9 only, remove 10s and face cards = 36 cards)
  • 1 place-value mat per explorer

How to Run

  1. Shuffle the deck. Deal 6 cards to each of the 4 explorers.
  2. Each explorer picks 5 of their 6 cards and arranges them to make the BIGGEST 5-digit number on their mat.
  3. Going around the crew, each explorer reads their number aloud ("Forty-seven thousand, two hundred eighty-six").
  4. Whoever has the biggest number wins the round — earns 1 doubloon (tally).
  5. Reshuffle. Play best of 7 rounds.
Variation: Switch to making the SMALLEST number partway through — flips the strategy and forces them to rethink place value.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Treasure Pile Estimator 💰

Materials

  • 4 tubs labeled "Pile A" through "Pile D"
  • Each tub: beans / beads / coins / pebbles (50–300 per tub)
  • 1 estimate-sheet per kid

How to Run

  1. Each explorer starts at a different pile.
  2. Without touching: WRITE your estimate on the sheet.
  3. Now count by grouping into 10s, then 100s. Write the actual count.
  4. Subtract: "off by ___."
  5. Rotate piles every ~3 min so each kid touches all 4 piles by the end.
What to watch for: Kids who never group — they're counting one at a time and losing track. Pause them with: "What if you made small groups of 10?"
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Number Detective 🧐

Materials

  • 4 small whiteboards + dry-erase markers
  • Your prepared list of 8 numbers to call aloud

How to Run

  1. Numbers to call: 234,567 · 890,103 · 456,789 · 105,030 · 700,089 · 632,500 · 90,005 · 348,902
  2. Read a number aloud, slowly, ONCE. All 4 race to write the digits on their whiteboards.
  3. Ask: "What's the value of the 4 in your number?" Pause and hear all 4 answers.
  4. Ask: "What place is the 0 in?" Pause again.
  5. Discuss disagreements together — the small group makes this safe.
What to watch for: Listen to the "how did you know?" answers. Kids who reason place-value-by-position are strong. Kids who guess will need targeted reinforcement tomorrow.
2
WEEK 1 · DAY 2 · ISLAND

Reading the Tide

Rounding to 10, 100, 1,000
🌊
1
10 min Game

Tide Roller 🎲

Materials

  • 4 dice per explorer
  • 1 score sheet per player
  • Pencil per player

How to Run

  1. Each explorer rolls all 4 dice.
  2. Arrange the digits to make any 4-digit number.
  3. Round 1: round to nearest 10. Round 2: nearest 100. Round 3: nearest 1,000.
  4. Each correct rounded answer = 1 doubloon.
  5. Compare doubloons after 5 rolls each. Highest wins.
Trick to teach: Underline the digit you're rounding TO. Look just to the RIGHT.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

The Number Line Walk 🚶

Materials

  • Large floor number line (painter's tape on floor, marked 0–100 by 10s)
  • Stack of cards 0–99 (printed or handwritten)

How to Run

  1. Each explorer draws a card (e.g., "47").
  2. Walk to that spot on the floor number line.
  3. Look LEFT and RIGHT: are you closer to 40 or 50?
  4. Walk to the nearest 10. That's your rounded answer.
  5. Discuss the "close call" cases (like 45, exactly between). Repeat with new cards.
Why physical works: Rounding is about DISTANCE. When kids actually walk it, they feel whether 47 is closer to 50 or 40 — not just memorize a rule.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Tide Chart Riddles 🌊

Materials

  • 4 whiteboards + markers
  • Printed riddle set (below)

How to Run

  1. Riddle 1: "The tide is at 3,672 mm. The rocks stay dry only when the tide rounds DOWN to 3,000 mm. Are the rocks dry?"
  2. Riddle 2: "Your treasure pile has 4,499 coins. Round to nearest 1,000. Are you a millionaire?"
  3. Riddle 3: "You sailed 826 km yesterday and 174 km today. Round each to nearest 100, then add."
  4. Riddle 4: "Crew estimated catch at 6,500 fish. Actual is 6,378. Was the estimate close?"
  5. Read each aloud; all 4 race to write their rounded answer; discuss.
What to watch for: Kids who freeze on word problems benefit from saying the math aloud first: "What's the question asking?" then "What numbers do I have?"

Building the Shelter

Multi-digit addition & subtraction
🪵
1
10 min Game

Lumber Yard Bingo 🪓

Materials

  • 4 printed bingo cards (different layouts, 16 sums/diffs each)
  • Stack of 30 addition/subtraction cards (problems)
  • Beans or chips for marking

How to Run

  1. Each explorer gets a different bingo card. You are the "caller."
  2. Flip an addition or subtraction card and read aloud: "348 + 567."
  3. Everyone solves on their whiteboard.
  4. If your bingo card has the answer (915), mark it!
  5. First explorer to mark 4 in a row wins. Then play another round.
You are the caller: No need to designate someone — you can also pause to coach when someone struggles. Win-win.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Log Counter Shelter 🏚️

Materials

  • 1 set of base-10 blocks (divided among the 4 explorers)
  • "Shelter Challenge" sheet (printed)
  • Graph paper

How to Run

  1. Read the first challenge: "You need 348 logs. You have 142. How many more?"
  2. Each explorer builds 142 with blocks. Then they BUILD UP to 348.
  3. Count the new blocks added — that's the answer (206).
  4. Compare answers; discuss who built fastest and why.
  5. Try the next challenge.
Why this builds intuition: Subtraction-as-building shows kids that "how many more" is the same as the GAP between two numbers.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

The Carry Coach 👨‍🏫

Materials

  • 4 whiteboards + markers
  • List of problems below

How to Run

  1. Problem 1: 905 − 437 (borrow across zero is tricky!)
  2. Problem 2: 2,000 − 1,357 (borrow ALL the way)
  3. Problem 3: 4,567 + 8,945 (multiple carries)
  4. Story: "We have 1,847 coconuts. Crew ate 729. How many left?"
  5. Work each one as a group; pause to discuss strategies.
What to watch for: Kids who skip the borrow when subtracting from a zero. Pause them: "What number do you turn the 0 into when you borrow?" (10.)

Crab Catch Multiplication

Multi-digit multiplication
🦀
1
10 min Game

Crab Trap Roll 🎲

Materials

  • 2 dice per explorer (different colors)
  • 1 score sheet per player

How to Run

  1. Each explorer rolls both dice.
  2. Multiply the two numbers — that's how many crabs your trap caught.
  3. Write the multiplication and the answer on your sheet.
  4. Best of 10 rolls. Most crabs wins.
  5. Extension: roll 3 dice and multiply 2 of them. Pick which two to MAXIMIZE crabs!
Differentiation: For stronger explorers, swap 6-sided dice for 10-sided dice, or multiply 3 dice at once.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Crab Array Drawings 📐

Materials

  • Graph paper (1 sheet per kid)
  • Colored pencils or markers
  • List of multiplications to draw

How to Run

  1. Pick a multiplication: e.g., 6 × 24.
  2. Draw a rectangle on graph paper that's 6 squares tall and 24 squares wide.
  3. Count the squares — that's the answer!
  4. Color in the answer in a fun pattern (each row a different color).
  5. Move to the next multiplication. Compare drawings: same shape?
Why arrays: Kids who SEE multiplication as a rectangle (length × width) build a deeper understanding than rote times-tables. The visual stays with them.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Big Catch Coach & the Box Method 👨‍🏫

Materials

  • 4 whiteboards + markers
  • Pre-drawn 2x2 box-method grids on each board

How to Run

  1. Problem 1: 23 × 15 (box method walkthrough together)
  2. Problem 2: 34 × 18 (kids try; you coach)
  3. Word: "6 islands × 24 beaches each = ?"
  4. Challenge: 47 × 26
  5. Work each; emphasize partial-products and place-value reasoning.
What to watch for: The box method exposes which kids understand place value, and which are just memorizing "carry the 1." Push for understanding over speed.

The Compass Quest

BOSS DAY — All Week 1 skills combined
⚔️
1
10 min Game

Compass Treasure Hunt 🧭

Materials

  • 20 problem cards (mix of all 4 skills from Days 1-4)
  • 5 small "Compass Piece" tokens hidden around the room
  • Map of the room with hidden-location clues

How to Run

  1. The crew works TOGETHER. Pick 5 problem cards.
  2. Solve each one — the answer reveals a location clue (e.g., "457 = behind the blue book").
  3. The crew races to find the hidden compass pieces.
  4. 5 correct answers = 5 compass pieces found = mission complete!
  5. Celebrate finds with a quick group cheer.
Setup tip: Hide compass pieces somewhere kids can find them in 90 seconds (behind chairs, under books). The math, not the hunt, is the focus.

15 min Hands-On Build

Island Map Builder 🗺️

Materials

  • Large blank paper (poster size)
  • Rulers and colored pencils
  • "Island facts" sheet (uses all Week 1 skills)

How to Run

  1. Read the Island Facts sheet aloud (e.g., "The volcano is 4,832 meters tall").
  2. Each explorer rounds each fact to the nearest 100 on their own sheet.
  3. Together, draw a shared map of the island showing each feature.
  4. Add a scale: 1 cm = 100 m.
  5. Each kid signs the map.
Why this is great: Combines rounding (Day 2), measurement (Days 3-4), and storytelling. Plus, you get cool art to display.

Stone of Equivalence

Equivalent fractions (1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8)
🪨
1
10 min Game

Equivalence Match 🃏

Materials

  • Printable card set: 24 fraction cards, 12 pairs of equivalents
  • (e.g., 1/2 ↔ 4/8, 2/3 ↔ 6/9, 3/4 ↔ 9/12)

How to Run

  1. Shuffle. Lay all 24 cards face-down in a 4×6 grid.
  2. Going around the crew, each explorer flips 2 cards.
  3. If the fractions are equivalent, they keep the pair and go again.
  4. If not, flip them back. Memory + math!
  5. Most pairs at the end wins. Play 2 rounds.
Variation: For struggling kids, allow them to use fraction strips to test equivalence before claiming a pair.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Fraction Strip Quarry 📏

Materials

  • 1 set fraction strips per explorer (halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths, twelfths)
  • Equivalence record sheet

How to Run

  1. Each explorer takes one half-strip and lays it on the table.
  2. Find which other strips, lined up end-to-end, exactly match the half.
  3. Write the equivalence: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 6/12.
  4. Repeat for 1/3, 2/3, 3/4, 5/6.
  5. Compare findings at the end — same equivalences?
What to watch for: Kids who guess equivalence numerically without checking visually. Make them line up the strips every time.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Stone Riddles 🧐

Materials

  • 4 small whiteboards + dry-erase markers
  • Riddle list below

How to Run

  1. Read riddle 1 aloud: "I have a half-stone. My friend has 4 of the same shape. How many of my friend's stones equal my stone? Answer: 2."
  2. All 4 race to write the equivalent fraction on their boards.
  3. Reveal answers simultaneously. Discuss disagreements.
  4. Move to riddle 2: "Two-thirds of the temple is sand. Write this with sixths." Answer: 4/6.
  5. Continue with 4 more: 3/4 = ?/8, 5/6 = ?/12, 1/3 = ?/9, 7/8 = ?/16.
What to watch for: Listen to the explanations. Kids who say "I multiplied top and bottom by the same number" understand the rule. Kids who guess need more strip practice.

WEEK 2 · DAY 2 · TEMPLE

Hall of Mirrors

Comparing fractions with < > =
🪞
1
10 min Game

Fraction War ⚔️

Materials

  • 1 deck of cards (Ace-10, where Ace = 1)
  • Pencil and tally sheet

How to Run

  1. Deal the deck evenly to all 4 explorers.
  2. Each round: all 4 flip 2 cards — first card = numerator, second card = denominator.
  3. If numerator > denominator, flip another for denominator.
  4. Compare fractions. Biggest wins all 4 fractions.
  5. Play until one explorer has all cards, or 10 rounds (most wins).
Coaching moment: If two kids think their fractions are equal, have them prove it with fraction strips. Great teaching moment.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Floor Number Line 📐

Materials

  • Painter's tape: long line on floor (0 at one end, 1 at other)
  • Index cards with fractions: 1/4, 3/4, 1/2, 2/3, 5/8, 1/3, 7/8, 1/6

How to Run

  1. Lay the tape line on the floor. Mark 0 and 1.
  2. Each explorer gets 2 fraction cards and physically stands where they think their fractions go.
  3. Once all 4 have stood, the crew judges: are they in the right order?
  4. Discuss disagreements. Move kids as needed.
  5. Reshuffle and repeat with new cards.
Why this works: Physical movement makes the comparison concrete. Kids "feel" that 3/4 is closer to 1 than 1/2 is.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Mirror Riddles 🪞

Materials

  • 4 small whiteboards + dry-erase markers
  • 5 comparison problems below

How to Run

  1. Riddle 1: "You ate 3/8 of a pizza. I ate 1/2. Who ate more? Why?"
  2. Riddle 2: "5/6 or 4/5 — which is bigger? Explain."
  3. Riddle 3: "Order from least to greatest: 2/3, 1/2, 5/8."
  4. Riddle 4: "Is 7/10 closer to 1/2 or to 1?"
  5. Riddle 5: "True or false: 3/4 = 9/12. Prove it."
Push their thinking: Ask "is there a benchmark like 1/2 or 1 that could help?" Benchmarks beat common denominators when speed matters.

WEEK 2 · DAY 3 · TEMPLE

Adding Pool

Adding fractions with like and unlike denominators
💧
1
10 min Game

Pool Race 🎲

Materials

  • 8 dice (2 per explorer)
  • Pool sheet: blank fraction circle worth 1 whole, divided into 12 wedges

How to Run

  1. Each explorer has a pool sheet (a 12-wedge circle).
  2. Roll 2 dice. Smaller = numerator, bigger = denominator. (Re-roll if numerator > denominator.)
  3. Convert to twelfths and color in that many wedges on your pool.
  4. Roll again. Add the new fraction. Color it in.
  5. First to fill the whole pool (12/12) wins!
Variation: Use the rule "if you go over 12/12 you spill and lose half your wedges." Adds risk and forces careful adding.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Fraction Strip Adding 🧱

Materials

  • Fraction strips per explorer
  • Worksheet with 8 addition problems

How to Run

  1. Each explorer takes a worksheet: 1/4 + 1/2 = ?, 1/3 + 1/6 = ?, etc.
  2. Build each addition with strips: place a 1/4 strip + a 1/2 strip end-to-end.
  3. Find which single set of equal strips matches the total (e.g., 3/4).
  4. Write the sum.
  5. Move to next. 8 problems total.
What to watch for: Kids who add numerators AND denominators (1/4 + 1/2 = 2/6). Strips immediately show why that's wrong.
3
10 min Coach & Challenge

Pool Problems 🏊

Materials

  • 4 small whiteboards + dry-erase markers
  • 5 word problems below

How to Run

  1. Problem 1: "You filled 1/3 of the temple pool. Your friend added another 1/6. How much is full?"
  2. Problem 2: "3/8 of the pool is from rain, 1/4 is from a spring. Total?"
  3. Problem 3: "2/5 + 1/5 + 1/10 = ?"
  4. Problem 4: "Two-thirds of a barrel plus one-fourth of a barrel?"
  5. Problem 5: "You need 7/8 of a pool. You have 1/2 + 1/4. Are you there yet?"
Coach the strategy: Always ask "do these denominators match?" first. If not, find a common one. Make this a chant.

WEEK 2 · DAY 4 · TEMPLE

Mixing Chamber

Subtracting fractions and mixed numbers
⚗️
1
10 min Game

Subtraction Dice 🎲

Materials

  • 8 dice (2 per explorer)
  • Score sheet with starting whole of 1 (= 12/12)

How to Run

  1. Each explorer starts with a "chamber" of 1 whole (12/12).
  2. Roll 2 dice: bigger = denominator, smaller = numerator. Convert to twelfths.
  3. Subtract that fraction from your chamber. Write the new amount.
  4. Keep rolling. First to empty (or go negative) loses.
  5. Last one standing wins!
Discussion moment: When someone has 3/12 left and rolls 1/2 (= 6/12), pause and ask: "Can you subtract? What happens?" Great way to surface mixed/improper thinking.
2
15 min Hands-On Build

Carving the Stone 🪓

Materials

  • Fraction circles (pie pieces)
  • Subtraction worksheet

How to Run

  1. Each explorer builds a "stone" from 1 whole circle.
